[How the Role of Book Cover Design Has Changed in the Age of Social Media by Madelyn Johnson](https://lithub.com/how-the-role-of-book-cover-design-has-changed-in-the-age-of-social-media/)

> In the before times, a cover served primarily as an interpretation of the text: a visual invitation intended to both entice readers and help them understand the book’s content. When working on a new cover, designers would ask themselves: What is this novel? What emotional experience does it promise? These were the questions they attempted to answer though their work.
> 
> Today, another question has quietly entered the designer’s studio: How does the reader of this book want to be perceived? Or more bluntly: Who do they want to be?
> 
> These questions do not replace the original brief, but they do increasingly compete with it, in a shift that has placed a new but largely unspoken pressure on book cover designers. A cover no longer simply needs to communicate genre or tone. It must also construct an identity that a reader of that book can inhabit. If book covers once translated text for their readers, they now operate in reverse: translating readers for their audiences. In this context, the old adage has been inverted from don’t judge a book by its cover to judge a reader by the covers on their books.
