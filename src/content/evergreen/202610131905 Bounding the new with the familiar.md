Read about it in [Inside the Box](/bookshelf/inside-the-box), but of course I’ve known of this idea from before. Like, when I wrote somewhere that, unimaginable is usually imaginable, truly unimaginable things are impossible to imagine. I read about a similar idea in [Abundance](/bookshelf/klein-ezra-and-derek-thompson-abundance-how-we-build-a-better-future), where they talked about who gets funded for new research - it’s not truly new research, it’s familiar research. Though the story was different there.

This basically means throwing a rope to the reader (for example) when you’re doing truly unique work. So the wilder the setting, the more the plot needs to be something we already know - like the hero’s journey for example.

This is also a factor in other domains like technology where ‘robust design’ is for example used to describe features that help the intended audience place the new thing in their existent world.
