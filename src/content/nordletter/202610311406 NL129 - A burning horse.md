The route that I take every day takes me past the highway to a nature trail next to a large body of water. The trail sways, meanders. You cannot see to the end, or even the water, the first time you get on the trail. The trail is dark - it has gotten darker now. There are lamps overhead, but it's dark when we go out to walk. And it's quiet. You may see one or two people walk past you, or walking some distance ahead of you. Usually, cyclists pass us by. 

![A bend](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/10/nl129-05.jpeg)*A bend*

On Friday, I had gone for the walk alone. Prerna was tired, Savya looked sleepy. So, I went alone. 

I had walked past the big red building to my left, past the large trees and took a right near the bridge, when I saw the first group of people walk toward me. 

They were a family, or two families. There were kids with bright lights, in jackets, laughing, walking. 

I did not think much of it, but as I walked past the next bend, I could see more people - a crowd walking toward me. I saw people with lights on the other side, walking closer to the apartments that dot this area. And I thought, are they going trick or treating?

It was the Halloween evening after all. 

---

Halloween is not too big here. The weather does not allow people - children specifically - to walk around in costumes. You would have to put a jacket on your dress, which defeats the purpose. 

There are events in malls, of course. 

In Matinkylä, in our old building, before Halloween, they would put a notice on the board, that children would go trick or treating and if you don't want to participate, you should put this sticker on your door. 

As I said, not a big thing here. 

---

Savya had a Halloween celebration at his päiväkoti in the morning. I had gotten him a skeleton dress just last night. Prerna had painted his face. 

![Scary Savya](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/10/nl129-01.jpeg)*Scary Savya*

When I dropped him at his päiväkoti, children in his unit seemed happy to see him. The girls were dressed in their princess costumes. The boys were dressed in zombie costumes. Savya seemed to fit in perfectly. 

I so wanted to be a fly on the wall that day - to see what he did all day, what did they do for Halloween, how the children played?

I didn't, of course. I logged in and worked.

---

That's why when I saw the crowds on the trail, I thought they were out trick or treating. Some had bags with them. I felt a little sad that Savya was not out here today. I wanted to figure out where do they announce these things. I would love to bring Savya around for this. 

Prerna needs to build this app. 

As I walked some more, the crowds swelled, and after another turn, I could see the source of the commotion.

[Finland celebrated All Saints Day and Halloween on the same day](https://yle.fi/a/74-20254162?origin=rss). I had read the report in Yle and everything clicked for me. 

Something (I thought it looked like a horse) was burning at the beach. There was a crowd taking pictures, and looking at it. From a distance, I could see the lights coming out of what looked like a small pop-up shop. Perhaps something selling these lights the kids had. 

![So many people](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/10/nl129-03.jpeg)*People and a shop*

It wasn't.

It was a fire truck! Now I really missed having Savya here. He loves fire trucks! And vehicles in other and all forms. 

I appreciated that even at celebrations, Finnish people and institutions care about safety so much! 

I left with a few pictures of my own and a smile on my face. 

![Full moon and people](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/10/nl129-04.jpeg)*Full moon and people*

---

After getting home, I googled, and found out that the horse was a goat. What I had witnessed was the [annual autumn harvest festival](https://www.pientenhelsinki.fi/en/all-saints-day-in-helsinki-2023/). 

From Google:

> The festival revives _Kekri_, the ancient Finnish harvest festival that marked the end of the agricultural year and the beginning of the dark winter months. A massive, wheel-mounted _Kekripukki_ (Kekri goat) equipped with smoke machines and lights is paraded down to the water's edge. It is set ablaze to light up the autumn night, symbolising leaving behind the old year.

Good to know for next year.
