## Do you game?

Even if you don’t, you may have heard of the event that is [[202611182136 GTA VI|GTA VI]]. [[202611182136 GTA VI|GTA VI]] is so big that it has already made [3.8 billion in its first few days](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9zr5nyv5dj2o). For comparison, Avengers Endgame has made [around 3 billion in its entire run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films). GTA VI is so big that many networks, [including in Finland](https://yle.fi/a/74-20257918) reported massive traffic spikes when it launched. [[202611182136 GTA VI|GTA VI]] is so big that Prerna asked me about it.

[[202611182136 GTA VI|GTA VI]] is the follow up to the massively successful GTA V, which is the third highest selling game of all time, [behind Tetris and Minecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_video_games). The game released on November 19th. I had preloaded the game before that. But I could not be up at 12:00, waiting for the clock to count down to zero. There are videos of people doing it on socials. I have seen them.

![GTA](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/11/nl132-03.jpeg)

I started playing it the next day (or same day) after work. In fact I was up, playing it till 01:00 in the night. And then I had to wake up the next day at 07:00 and hit the gym.

The game is everything I expected it to be and more.

There have been just hints of the love story so far and I'm so damn excited for it. Like I have fixed the steps of the house, fixed the bedroom, painted the walls, moved the furniture around and fixed the roof. I fixed the roof after I took a cab home and did not have money to pay for it. The cabbie called the cops so I ran to my house, got to the roof and waited. The cops came, did not find me and went away after a bit. I saw then that there was work to be done on the roof, so I just got to it. After finishing the work, I got down and hit the bed.

I am doing things a bit similar to RDR2 wherein I'm enjoying the routine of the thing. I'm treating Jason as a human. After waking up, I workout, have a protein shake, take a shower, change and then pick one or two missions to do. I get home, spend some time with Lucia, then sleep. 

Also, the game and the missions are designed in such a way that these things don't feel like a chore. Like I want to do the side missions. You know? Like getting rum for my neighbour. I did not feel the same way in [[202608202205 Spiderman 2|Spiderman 2]], for example. I do want to get a new car though. I don't like the one I have now. Though I assume that I will get a better one as the game progresses. 

Also, in RDR2 you could call out to your horse and it would get to where you are - a similar feature existed for Cyberpunk 2077. I find that I miss it here. Like I would go on a mission, park the car somewhere, and then have to drive or sail all the way back to the parked car to get it back. The car does not teleport to where I am. I do like the mechanic of stowing stuff in your car. That's good and feels way more natural.

Are you playing GTA? I have not been this excited to play a game in years. I used to feel this way while playing Resident Evil in school. I would think about how to beat that chainsaw-wielding monster in the village while in class. And then go home and do it.

## Units of work

[[202611141416 NL131 - Post Diwali Blues|After a week spent recuperating]], I spent most of last week getting back to my routine - studying for CKA, working out, writing haikus. 

> White fluffy snow falls.
> A dog and a child stroll on the grass.
> I sit writing at my desk.

Haikus are more than poems, more than the 5-7-5 syllables. They capture a moment in time. Most of them happen while I am *in* the moment, or just outside. Haikus allow me to sometimes think back to a moment worth writing about, in the day that was. (A similar idea to [[202602221707 Two sentence journals|Two sentence journals]].) 

Purely in writing terms though, each haiku may be small, but if I keep at it, then eventually I will have a collection of these moments I could decide to publish. 

[Craig Mod](https://craigmod.com/essays/to_make_a_book_walk_on_a_book/) writes in a similar way - writing multiple essays (newsletters) at the end of each day of walking - thousands of words - and then collecting them in a book.

I liked thinking of work this way - in terms of atomic units that you could actually work on, instead of large units you fantasise about working on.

Dividing work into smaller manageable units makes it doable.

I finished reading [[202610101315 Obit|Obit]] this week. 

![Obit](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/11/nl132-01.jpeg)*Obit*

I had read a third of it and then did not touch it till today. It just sat on the shelf. Waiting.

Then I read [[202611140059 Writing a poetry collection as a page turner|writing a poetry collection as a page turner on LitHub]],

>Think of Victoria Chang’s [_Obit_](https://bookshop.org/p/books/obit-victoria-chang/50cdf688c8b1e991?ean=9781556595745&affiliate=132), where each poem functions as an obituary for the human and nonhuman, literal and abstract;

And I thought, hey, I have this book. It's funny how recommendations from strangers make you appreciate something more. So I picked it up, brought it down to Oodi with me, and then finished it before writing my own things.

Obit is a beautiful book. Poems (most of them) are written in the shape of a tombstone - a block of rectangular text in the middle of the page. It is a beautiful thing to hold in your hand. Each poem begins with something dying - hope, home, America.

I think I will buy it and keep it in my library.

![A poem from the book](https://storage.sajalchoudhary.net/images/2026/11/nl132-02.jpeg)*A poem from the book*

Also, what is the best way to read a poetry book? To perhaps capture some of what it made you feel? I highlighted, took some sections that spoke to me in the moment. If I look at them after a certain period of time has passed, will they feel the same way?

They don't even feel that way now. Five minutes after. They feel out of context. And they are. The words feel different when read in a poem, than outside.
