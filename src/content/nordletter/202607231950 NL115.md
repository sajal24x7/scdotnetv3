I finished reading [[202607201343 The Courage to be Disliked|The Courage to be Disliked]] this week.

Someone at the TCS Bibliography Group had mentioned that this had been a life-changing book for them. And I thought, if that does not make it a worthy recommendation, what else can. On the first day, when I started reading it, I realised the assertion that this was a life-changing book was correct, and it would be a life-changing book for me as well.

**The Courage to be Disliked** is written by two Japanese authors, based on [Alfred Adler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Adler)'s philosophy. 

I found myself taking many notes as I read this book, but the work of thinking about these things remains. That is the whole point after all. To convert the author's thoughts, assimilate them in my mind and with my thoughts. To write about the concepts in my words. To see if they make sense to me or not. To see how I could incorporate the philosophies in my life.

One of the core ideas for me at least was this - we need to recognise what our tasks are, and what someone else's tasks are. We need to separate the tasks. And then we need to work on our tasks. This was explained in terms of raising an unruly child - someone who refuses to study. Most parents would worry about that, scold them, do their work. But studying is the child's task. You can help them, sure, but you can not study for them. It's their task. 

As they say, you can take a horse to the water, but not drink the water.

So, try and give it a read.
