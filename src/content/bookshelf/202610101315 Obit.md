## Review

I picked up this book as inspiration for the work I wanted to do next - a poetry collection of my own for my mother. I had picked up [Native Guard](/bookshelf/native-guard) for the same reason.

I had read a third of it and then did not touch it till today. It just sat on the shelf. Waiting.

Then I read [writing a poetry collection as a page turner on LitHub](/micro/writing-a-poetry-collection-as-a-page-turner),

>Think of Victoria Chang’s [_Obit_](https://bookshop.org/p/books/obit-victoria-chang/50cdf688c8b1e991?ean=9781556595745&affiliate=132), where each poem functions as an obituary for the human and nonhuman, literal and abstract;

And I thought, hey, I have this book. So I picked it up again today, on this Saturday and brought it with me to Oodi. To read before writing today. I read and wrote two poems today. Not the writing I had planned to do. That is yet to commence. 

It is a a beautiful book. Poems (most of them) are written in the shape of a tomb stone - a block of rectangular text in the middle of the page. It is a beautiful thing to hold in your hand. Each poem begins with something dying - hope, home, america.

What is the best way to read a poetry book. To perhaps capture some of what it made you feel? I highlighted, took some sections that spoke to me in the moment. If I look at them after a certain period of time has past, will they feel the same way?

They don't even feel that way now. Five minutes after. They feel out of context. And they are. The words feel different when read in a poem, than outside.

Did I say it is a beautiful book? I feel lucky to have picked it in its physical form. I think that's a good aim to have. To have a physical book feel different and perhaps more than just the words that are on paper.

I think I will buy it and keep it in my library. I loved it that much!

## Notes

> My mother pointed at me, / want to live longer so I can spend more time with her. I think she meant my children, what I represented.

> I blame God. I want to complain to the boss of God about God. What if the boss of God is rain and the only way to speak to rain is to open your mouth to the sky and drown?

> Even as she was dying, she thought the path to God was money.

> Imagination is having to live in a dead person's future. Grief is wearing a dead person's dress forever.

> God suddenly cuts in front of you creating a hazard. Which of these actions should you take first? a Honk and step on the brake firmly, b) Take your foot off the gas, c Swerve into the lane next to you. Is there a choice d? Accelerate and run God over.

I laughed at this. In a book where I have just been on the verge of crying.
